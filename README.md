# carbon-cost

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.24396111.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24396111)

What carbon and road charges does a truck trip pay? carbon-cost works it out per country and per charge, and shows the carbon part separately:

- **Km-based road tolls:** Denmark (vejafgift), Germany (Lkw-Maut), Austria (GO-Maut), the Netherlands (vrachtwagenheffing), Belgium (kilometerheffing, by region), Switzerland and Liechtenstein (LSVA), Iceland (kílómetragjald, on all roads, the tractor and the trailer each by its own mass: give `motor_axles`, `motor_mass_kg` and `trailer_mass_kg`), Belarus (BelToll), Czechia (mýtné), Poland (e-TOLL), Bulgaria (BG TOLL), Slovenia (DarsGo), Slovakia (mýto), Hungary (HU-GO) and Romania (TollRo), split into their legal parts (infrastructure, air, noise and CO2) where the law gives them.
- **Entry-to-exit motorway tolls:** Croatia (Hrvatske autoceste and Bina-Istra), Serbia (Putevi Srbije), Montenegro (Monteput) and France (APRR, AREA, ADELAC, ALIAE, VINCI Autoroutes' ASF, Escota and Cofiroute, Sanef and SAPN, A'liénor, the Millau viaduct, ATMB's A40, Atlandes, ALBEA, SFTRF's A43, Alicorne, ALiS and the Normandie and Tancarville bridges), priced from the station where the route enters a toll system to the one where it leaves.
- **Time-based road user charges:** Latvia (autoceļu lietošanas nodeva), Lithuania (kelių naudotojo mokestis), Estonia (teekasutustasu), the Eurovignette in Sweden (vägavgift) and Luxembourg (droit d'usage), and the UK HGV levy, priced per day the trip spends in the country by its passage times (a calendar day in Sweden, Luxembourg and the UK, 24 hours in Estonia and Lithuania; Latvia at one day). Lithuania counts the motor vehicle's own axles: give `motor_axles` (e.g. 2 for a tractor).
- **Motorway sections charged per passage:** Portugal's lanços (IMT's table), each in its middle.
- **Toll stations charged per passage:** Norway's AutoPASS stations (bompenger), in the direction each charges, at rush-hour prices in rush hours and once per hour-rule group; Ireland's toll plazas (M50 and the PPP roads); Sweden's bridge charges (Motala, Sundsvall, Skuru) and the Stockholm and Göteborg congestion taxes by time of day; North Macedonia's toll stations; Montenegro's Sozina tunnel; Greece's motorway toll stations (Olympia Odos, Nea Odos, Kentriki Odos, Moreas, Aegean Motorway) and the Rio–Antirrio bridge; Turkey's KGM bridges (15 Temmuz Şehitler, Fatih Sultan Mehmet, Osmangazi, Yavuz Sultan Selim).
- **Bridge tolls:** Storebælt and Øresund, a fixed price per crossing by the truck's length (`length_m`, default 16.5 m).
- **Ferry fares** (`ferry_fare`): Scandlines (Puttgarden–Rødby, Rostock–Gedser, Helsingør–Helsingborg), POLSCA's Baltic ferries, Bornholmslinjen and the Danish domestic ferries, the Elbe ferry Glückstadt–Wischhafen, Estonia's state ferries, Norway's national road ferries, Jadrolinija, the Strait of Messina and CalMac, from their published freight tariffs, when the route takes the crossing.
- **Area charges per day** (`area_charge`): London's Congestion Charge, when the route is in the zone in its charging hours (with a departure `time`; without one, assumed).
- **Tunnels and bridges charged per passage**, on top of the countries' own tolls: the Herrentunnel (Lübeck), the Warnowtunnel (Rostock), the Westerscheldetunnel, the Liefkenshoektunnel (Antwerp), the Great St Bernard tunnel, the Dartford Crossing, the Tyne Tunnels, the M6 Toll, the Humber, Mersey and Tamar bridges and the Kiltunnel, each in the direction it charges.
- **Electronic toll discounts:** give `etc` when the truck pays by toll box or account agreement: Croatia's ENC (21.74%, plus 3% EURO V/EEV or 7% EURO VI on HAC; 30% on Bina-Istra), Storebælt's Grøn rabat (13%, EURO VI or zero-emission), and ØresundBUSINESS prices.
- **EU ETS on ferry crossings:** the cargo's share of the crossing's emissions × the allowance price.
- **ETS2 on road fuel** from 2028: the CO2 of the truck's diesel × the ETS2 price.
- **National CO2 prices on diesel** (`fuel_co2`): Germany's BEHG (nEHS), Austria's NEHG, Ireland's carbon tax, Portugal's adicionamento sobre as emissões de CO2 and Slovenia's CO2 okoljska dajatev, on the CO2 of the truck's diesel, and the CO2 taxes per litre of Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland, Lithuania, Luxembourg and Iceland, and Poland's emission fee per litre, in the country where the fuel is bought (`fuel_purchases` or `fuel_country`, else split by each country's road km).

The route, the km per country and the emissions come from [Sootcase](https://github.com/majidkabir/sootcase) (GLEC Framework and ISO 14083), or from the route your truck drove. The rates come from the laws, and each rate file names the law and the page it uses. There are no fitted factors: every number is a published rate, a price you give, your truck's own data, Sootcase's result, or, for the diesel when you give none, the European Commission's figure for a new truck of its group.

A trip can have several stops and shipments: the truck pays the trip, and each shipment its share by tonne-km, the method of EN ISO 14083 and the EU's CountEmissionsEU regulation (see [Trips and shipments](#trips-and-shipments)).

## Example

```
$ carbon-cost calc -from DKCPH -to DEHAM -cargo-kg 20000 -mass-kg 40000 -axles 5 -euro 6 -co2-class 1 -eua 75
Trip on 2026-11-03: 300.0 km (19.0 km by ferry)
     Charge  Country            Part         Basis           Rate     Amount    EUR
  road_toll       DE  infrastructure      141.0 km   0.155 EUR/km  21.86 EUR  21.86
  road_toll       DE             air      141.0 km   0.023 EUR/km   3.24 EUR   3.24
  road_toll       DE           noise      141.0 km   0.012 EUR/km   1.69 EUR   1.69
  road_toll       DE             co2      141.0 km   0.158 EUR/km  22.28 EUR  22.28
  road_toll       DK  infrastructure      140.0 km    0.44 DKK/km  61.60 DKK   8.26
  road_toll       DK             co2      140.0 km     0.6 DKK/km  84.00 DKK  11.26
  road_toll       DK       air_noise      140.0 km    0.06 DKK/km   8.40 DKK   1.13
  ferry_ets                      co2  0.030 t CO2e  75 EUR/t CO2e   2.25 EUR   2.25
   fuel_co2       DE             co2  0.118 t CO2e   65 EUR/t CO2   7.66 EUR   7.66
   fuel_co2       DK             co2      140.0 km    2.284 DKK/l  100.05 DKK  13.41
Total: EUR 93.04, of which carbon EUR 56.86
Truck: 281.0 road km, 87.9 l diesel (default: VECTO group 5-LH (tractor with semi-trailer, long haul), by the load on each leg ...)
```

The `fuel_co2` lines are the national CO2 prices on diesel (Germany's BEHG, Denmark's CO2-afgift) on the truck's diesel: 87.9 l, a new long-haul tractor's 835 g CO2/km with 20 t on board (COM(2024) 313) over the 281 road km, taken as bought along the route in proportion to each country's road km. Give `-l-per-100km` or `-diesel-l` for your truck's own, and `-fuel-bought DE=300,DK=100` (or `-fuel-country`) for where it fills up. A live run gives a different total: Sootcase's real route and emissions replace this fixed 300 km trip.

The numbers are this repo's worked test case (`cost/cost_test.go`).

### Compare trucks

`-compare 1,4,5` prices the same trip for trucks in other CO2 classes, and `-trips-per-year` turns the savings per trip into savings per year:

```
$ carbon-cost calc -from DKCPH -to DEHAM -cargo-kg 20000 -eua 75 -compare 1,4,5 -trips-per-year 200
...
  CO2 class  Tolls EUR  Total EUR  Carbon EUR  Saving/trip  Saving/year
          1      69.72      93.04       56.86         0.00
          4      48.82      72.14       40.09        20.90      4180.00
          5       2.44       4.69        2.25        88.35     17670.00
```

The route and the cargo's emissions stay the same. The exception is a class 5 (zero-emission) truck, which burns no road fuel and so pays no fuel CO2 prices or ETS2. In 2026 the class 5 truck pays only the DK class 5 rate, because Germany exempts it.

## Rates included

carbon-cost keeps and prices every table below as an [ORCT](https://github.com/majidkabir/orct) 0.3 dataset (Open Road Charge Tariff), one per scheme and version, in [`tariffs/data`](tariffs/data) (`index.json` lists them), priced by its own ORCT calculator (`orct`). The workflows that fetch the sources build each table and convert it to ORCT right away (`tools/orct/convert.py`; the tables' fields in [TABLES.md](tools/orct/TABLES.md)); only the datasets are committed. The ORCT repo publishes them too, under [data/carbon-cost](https://github.com/majidkabir/orct/tree/main/data/carbon-cost).

## Trips and shipments

The method is [EN ISO 14083:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78864.html), which [Regulation (EU) 2026/1030](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1030/oj) (CountEmissionsEU, in force 1 June 2026) makes the EU's method for transport emissions. It works in two steps: the vehicle's trip, then each shipment's share of it by its tonne-km.

- **Stops** (`stops`, 2 to 25, instead of `from` and `to`; CLI `-via "DEBRE;NLRTM"` between `-from` and `-to`, `-return` to drive back empty): Sootcase routes each leg once, at the heaviest load on board, and its emissions are scaled to each leg's own load. A leg with nothing on board is empty running, part of the trip, and its km pay tolls like any other.
- **Shipments** (`shipments`: `id`, `weight_kg`, `from` and `to` as stop numbers, 0 the first; CLI `-shipments "A:12000:0-2;B:8000:1-2"`): the truck pays the trip's charges; each shipment's share is its weight × the km of its own route from its loading to its unloading stop (ISO 14083's transport activity distance), over all the shipments'. The response's `shipments` gives each one's km, tonne-km, share, amounts by charge and by country (`by_country`, for an invoice that passes the tolls on country by country), amounts per 100 kg (`eur_per_100kg`, as surcharges are quoted) and emissions; the trip's `eur_per_tkm` is every shipment's rate. Without `shipments`, `cargo_kg` is one shipment from the first stop to the last.
- **The truck's diesel** (`trip.fuel_l`, with `trip.fuel_basis` saying where it comes from), on which the CO2 prices on fuel and ETS2 are charged: `diesel_l` (litres burnt on the trip), else `l_per_100km` × the road km, else `fuel_purchases` (litres bought by country, `[{"country": "DE", "litres": 300}]`, CLI `-fuel-bought DE=300,PL=150`; with either of the others they only say where the diesel was bought and the CO2 prices are paid), else a new truck of its VECTO group with each leg's load: the European Commission's [report on new heavy-duty vehicles' CO2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52024DC0313) (COM(2024) 313, VECTO, reporting period 2021) gives the long-haul 4x2 tractor with semi-trailer (sub-group 5-LH) 632 g CO2/km at its low payload (2.6 t) and 826 g/km at its representative one (19.2 t), taken as linear in the load between and beyond them, and each other group's average; litres at 2.67 kg CO2 per litre (EN 16258:2012). A zero-emission truck (CO2 class 5) burns none.
- **Emissions** (`ttw_kg`, `wtw_kg` and `intensity_g_co2e_per_tkm`, for the trip and each shipment, with `data_type` and `emissions_basis`): with the truck's diesel given (`diesel_l`, `l_per_100km` or `fuel_purchases`) they are its litres × 2.67 kg CO2 tank-to-wheel and 3.24 kg CO2e well-to-wheel (EN 16258:2012, Table A.1) shared out by tonne-km, plus each ferry's (Sootcase's for the load on board, shared by weight among the shipments on it): `data_type` `primary`, the data Regulation (EU) 2026/1030 puts before default values. Otherwise they are Sootcase's GLEC default intensities × each shipment's tonne-km: `modelled`.
- **The route driven** (`path`, `[[lon, lat], ...]` from GPS or telematics, up to 200,000 points; CLI `-path track.gpx`, GPX or GeoJSON; the web page takes the file): tolls, crossings and km by country and region are priced on it instead of Sootcase's route, split into legs at the stops, with the same borders and rule Sootcase uses. The shipments' tonne-km and emissions stay on their own routes. A note says when the path passes far from a stop or takes a ferry Sootcase's route doesn't.
- **Km by country, with no route** (`country_km`, e.g. `{"DE": 320, "PL": 410, "BE-VLG": 60}`, from a tachograph; CLI `-country-km DE=320,PL=410`): per-km and per-day charges are priced; tolls at stations or on sections (France, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Croatia, ...), bridges, ferries and zones need a route and are listed in `missing` (reason `no_route`). The emissions are the diesel's: 2.67 kg CO2 per litre tank-to-wheel and 3.24 kg CO2e well-to-wheel (EN 16258:2012, Table A.1). Sootcase isn't called.

```
$ carbon-cost calc -from DEHAM -via DEBER -to DEMUC -return -shipments "A:12000:0-2;B:8000:1-2" -l-per-100km 29 -fuel-bought DE=400
```

## Things to know

- **Tolled roads:** Where the tool has no road classes for a country (see Road types), all its km are counted as tolled by default. In Denmark the 2025–2027 tolled network covers about 100% of foreign trucks' driving and about 70% of Danish trucks' ([Skatteministeriet's answer to Folketinget](https://eu.dk/samling/20222/lovforslag/l74/spm/35/svar/1958301/2708151.pdf)), and from 2028 every public road is tolled. In Germany and Austria the road classes tell the tolled km (DE A and B roads, AT A and S) from the rest. If part of a trip is on untolled roads, give `tolled_share`.
- **Regional tolls:** where a country's toll differs by region (Belgium), the tool prices the km per region from Sootcase's `region_km` (ISO 3166-2, e.g. `BE-VLG`). `country_km` still covers the whole country. `tolled_share` and `env_zone_km` take a region code or a country code. 
- **Environmental zones:** the Danish surcharge applies to the km you give in `env_zone_km`.
- **Ferry ETS, as the line charges it:** on a crossing that one line sails and that publishes its ETS surcharge for freight, the EU ETS is that surcharge × the truck's length (`length_m`, 16.5 m assumed), which is what the truck is charged, loaded or empty, whether or not you give `eua_eur_per_t`: DFDS's monthly surcharge per lane metre ([its page](https://www.dfds.com/en/freight-ferries-and-logistics/ets-surcharges), by the month's average EU allowance price), Scandlines' per commencing metre, at least 7, from its monthly surcharges notices (Rødby–Puttgarden, Gedser–Rostock), and POLSCA's per commenced metre from each announcement (Świnoujście–Ystad, –Trelleborg, Gdańsk–Nynäshamn, –Karlshamn), in [ferries/data/ets-surcharges.json](ferries/data/ets-surcharges.json), built by `tools/tables/ferry_ets.py`. A crossing more than one line sails (Dover–Calais, Świnoujście–Trelleborg) and every other crossing stay on the ships' emissions below, with a note giving the one line's surcharge. `ferry_ets_basis: "emissions"` prices every crossing on the ships' emissions.
- **Ferry ETS, from the ships' emissions:** the ETS cost of the cargo's share of each crossing. The EU ETS covers ships of 5,000 GT and above (Directive 2003/87/EC, Annex I): 100% of a voyage's emissions between ports under the jurisdiction of Member States (the EU and the EEA's Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein), 50% between one of them and a port outside (Art. 3ga(1)), and none while an island derogation is in force (Art. 12(3-d), Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/2895: Køge–Rønne until 2030 (Rønne is Bornholm's listed port), Destination Gotland's Nynäshamn–Visby and Oskarshamn–Visby until 2030 (Visby is Gotland's), the crossings OpenStreetMap has to the listed ports of Ibiza, Formentera and Menorca (Dénia–Eivissa, Barcelona–Maó, …), of Italian islands such as Elba, Ischia, Procida, Lipari or San Pietro (Piombino–Portoferraio, Pozzuoli–Ischia, Portovesme–Carloforte, …) of Gozo (Ċirkewwa–Mġarr), and of the Greek islands (Piraeus–Chios, Kyllini–Zakynthos, Igoumenitsa–Lefkimmi, Naxos–Paros, …: a port the list doesn't name, such as Corfu's town port, has no derogation) until 2030, Hou–Sælvig from 2025 until 2030, and Jadrolinija's crossings to and from the Croatian island ports it lists, such as Split–Supetar or Valbiska–Merag, and Rapska plovidba's Stinica–Mišnjak (Rab), until 2030; [ferries/data/crossings-islands.json](ferries/data/crossings-islands.json), built by `tools/tables/ets_islands.py` from the Decision's Official Journal text and Jadrolinija's lines). On a crossing the tool knows (Rødby–Puttgarden, Gedser–Rostock, Helsingør–Helsingborg, Hou–Sælvig, Bøjden–Fynshav, Esbjerg–Nordby, Virtsu–Kuivastu, Rohuküla–Heltermaa; [ferries/data/crossings.json](ferries/data/crossings.json), with the operators' pages naming the ships; and Bornholmslinjen's Køge–Rønne, Ystad–Rønne and Sassnitz–Rønne, Molslinjen's Odden–Aarhus and Odden–Ebeltoft, Destination Gotland's Nynäshamn–Visby and Oskarshamn–Visby (its ferries page names each ferry's route), POLSCA's Świnoujście–Ystad, Świnoujście–Trelleborg, Gdańsk–Nynäshamn and Gdańsk–Karlshamn, TT-Line's Travemünde–Trelleborg, Rostock–Trelleborg and Świnoujście–Trelleborg, Stena Line's routes (Gdynia–Karlskrona, Gothenburg–Kiel, Gothenburg–Frederikshavn, Nynäshamn–Ventspils, Travemünde–Liepāja, Trelleborg–Rostock, the North Sea and Irish Sea) and Finnlines' Travemünde–Malmö, Świnoujście–Malmö and Travemünde–Helsinki, [ferries/data/crossings-ships.json](ferries/data/crossings-ships.json), built by `tools/tables/ferry_ships.py` from Bornholmslinjen's ferries page, POLSCA's timetables and TT-Line's freight timetable ([read in Chrome](tools/tables/browser/ttline-timetable.txt), as the site refuses scripted requests), which name the ferry of each departure, and Stena Line Freight's, Finnlines', Irish Ferries Freight's and P&O Freight's route pages, which name each route's ferries, DFDS's timetables (its API, the ship and IMO number of each departure in the next 28 days, on the routes its freight pages list), Tallink's freight fleet page (each route's ships: Tallinn–Helsinki), Eckerö Line's cargo page (Helsinki–Tallinn, Vuosaari–Muuga), Color Line's routes page (Kiel–Oslo, Hirtshals–Larvik and –Kristiansand, Strömstad–Sandefjord), Fjord Line's ships page (Hirtshals–Kristiansand), Wasaline's cargo page (Vaasa–Umeå), the Port of Helsinki's Katajanokka terminal page (Viking Line's Viking XPRS to Tallinn) and Brittany Ferries freight's timetables (its API: each sailing's ship for the year ahead, on Portsmouth–Caen, –Cherbourg, –Saint-Malo, –Santander, –Bilbao, Plymouth–Roscoff, –Santander, Cork–Roscoff, Cherbourg–Rosslare, Bilbao–Rosslare) (Irish Ferries with their IMO numbers: Dublin–Holyhead, Dover–Calais, Rosslare–Pembroke, Dublin–Cherbourg; P&O on Dover–Calais, Hull–Rotterdam, Larne–Cairnryan and Tilbury–Zeebrugge, its Dover–Calais ferries from its sailing tables); and the ferry lines OpenStreetMap has between a port outside the EU and the EEA (the UK, the Isle of Man, the Channel Islands, the Faroes; Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Albania, Montenegro, Turkey) and one in the table, such as Dover–Calais, Portsmouth–Santander, Hirtshals–Tórshavn, Algeciras–Tanger Med or Bari–Durrës, whose ships aren't named but whose ports' countries give the share; a crossing between two UK ports is outside the EU ETS and under the UK ETS, below), the share follows from its ports and ships: a crossing whose ships are missing from the EU MRV reports since they entered service is under 5,000 GT and pays none (Esbjerg–Nordby; Virtsu–Kuivastu and Rohuküla–Heltermaa, TS Laevad's five ferries), as every ship above 5,000 GT on EU/EEA voyages reports (Regulation (EU) 2015/757, Art. 2(1)); the MRV reports' passenger, ro-pax and ro-ro ships are in `ferries/data/mrv-ships-<year>.json`. When you give the truck's `tare_kg`, the emissions come from the crossing's own ships in the EU MRV report: their freight CO2e per tonne-nautical mile × (truck + cargo) × the crossing's distance. MRV counts the vehicle in the freight mass, which is why the tare is needed. Otherwise the emissions come from Sootcase's ferry part, which uses the GLEC ro-pax default. Norway's fjord ferries aren't known crossings: no official source names each connection's ships (the national road database NVDB, Ferjedatabanken and Statens vegvesen's market overview give its operator and contract, and the operators' route pages give times and fares), so the size of the ships can't be told from the MRV reports. On a crossing the tool doesn't know, all its emissions count (`ferry_ets_share` sets 0.5 for a voyage to or from a port outside the EU/EEA), and a note says that small ships and derogations aren't known there. The phase-in is 40% for 2024, 70% for 2025 and 100% from 2026. A trip over several crossings gets a line for each. A ferry line's own surcharge is based on its ships' real emissions and its own pricing.
- **Ferry UK ETS:** on a crossing between two UK ports (Belfast–Cairnryan, –Heysham and –Liverpool, Cairnryan–Larne, the Northern Isles), from 1 July 2026, with `uka_gbp_per_t` (the UK allowance price, GBP per t CO2e; CLI `-uka`): the cargo's share of the crossing's emissions, as for the EU ETS, times the share the UK ETS covers (The Greenhouse Gas Emissions Trading Scheme Order 2020, Schedule 2A, as inserted by [SI 2026/392](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/392/made)): 100% of a voyage between UK ports and 50% of one between Northern Ireland and Great Britain (para 15(1)), none on a Scottish ferry service between points in Scotland (para 5(1)(k)) or a ship under 5,000 GT (para 5(1)). A port's UK nation is in its terminal's `region` (GB-NIR, GB-SCT). The UK ETS doesn't cover voyages to the Isle of Man or the Channel Islands, or international ones.
- **Times and directions:** with a departure `time` (local time where the trip starts; each station is timed in its own country's zone), each toll station is taken to be reached after its km along the route at `avg_speed_kmh` (default 70, an assumption, not a law). That time prices congestion taxes per passage (with their daily caps and Göteborg's 60-minute rule, which charges the highest amount once) and the hours a toll system or station is free. With `driver_rest`, the times include one driver's 45-minute break after 4.5 hours' driving and 11-hour daily rest after 9 hours ([Regulation (EC) No 561/2006](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/561/oj) Arts. 6–8); the web page turns it on. The times also count the days of time-based charges. Stations with a heading charge only the direction they face; entry–exit tariffs published per direction (Turkey's concessions, Spain's SEITT) are priced entry to exit, never the other way's price.
- **Fuel CO2:** national CO2 prices on diesel are paid where the fuel is bought, which the route doesn't tell. Give `fuel_purchases` or `fuel_country`; otherwise the diesel is split by each country's road km (an assumption). They are charged on the truck's diesel (see [Trips and shipments](#trips-and-shipments)): taxes per litre (DK, SE, NO, FI, LT, LU, IS, PL, AL) on its litres, prices per tonne of CO2 on its litres × 2.67 kg (EN 16258:2012).
- **Prices:** EUA and ETS2 prices are yours to give, since they move every day. DKK converts at the ERM II central rate of 7.46038 unless you give `dkk_per_eur`. CHF, CZK, PLN, HUF, RON, SEK, NOK, GBP and TRY convert at the latest [ECB euro reference rates](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html), RSD and ALL (which the ECB doesn't publish) at the [National Bank of Serbia's official middle rate](https://webappcenter.nbs.rs/ExchangeRateWebApp/ExchangeRate/CurrentMiddleRate) and the [Bank of Albania's official exchange rate](https://www.bankofalbania.org/Markets/Official_exchange_rate/) of the day (embedded, `fx/data/nbs-eur-rsd.csv` and `fx/data/boa-eur-all.csv`, built daily by the History data workflow and published by Publish data when only new days were added), unless you give `fx` (units per euro, e.g. `{"CHF": 0.93}`).
- **Road types:** Czechia, Poland, Bulgaria, Slovakia, Hungary and Romania charge less off motorways and don't toll local roads; Germany (Bundesautobahnen and Bundesstraßen, BFStrMG § 1, not the A 6 and A 5 border stretches of § 1 (3) nor the privately tolled Warnow and Herren tunnels) and Austria (Autobahnen and Schnellstraßen, BStMG § 1) charge one rate on their tolled roads and nothing off them. The tool reads the road class of each km of the route from OpenStreetMap (embedded in `roads/data`, built by `tools/roads`): the toll law's motorway class (CZ D, BG A, SK D and R, HU M, RO A and DEx), other national roads (1–2 digit refs; RO DN), and local roads, which aren't charged. Poland's e-TOLL charges only the sections its regulation lists (Dz. U. 2026 poz. 33, annex 1 for motorways and expressways, annex 2 for other national roads): `tools/roads/pl_network.py` reads them from the law and `tools/roads/plnet.py` finds each between its junctions, borders, city borders and crossings in OpenStreetMap; the rest, including the concession motorways A1 Rusocin–Toruń, A2 Świecko–Konin and A4 Katowice–Kraków, which their operators charge instead, and national roads in cities with county rights, isn't charged by e-TOLL. Hungary's air and noise charge also takes each km's suburban share from its toll section (OpenStreetMap relations tagged `ref:HU:edid`). `motorway_share` and `tolled_share` override this. A route point counts as on a road within 40 m of it; km counted as local that pass within 150 m of a tolled road are reported in a note, since they may be on it.
- **Checked against the official calculators:** the Czech route calculator (myto.gov.cz, Praha–Brno, 182.97 km of D1: CZK 1 091.31) gives the tool's total to the haléř (`TestOfficialCalculators`); Poland's e-TOLL calculator prices Wrocław–Gliwice at PLN 80.15 and the tool, on its own route, at PLN 80.25. The calculators' pages, as read, are in `tools/tables/browser`.
- **Past dates:** `date` picks the tables in force that day, and euro rates are the ECB's for that day (embedded since 2024; the dinar's, the NBS's; the lek's, the Bank of Albania's). Rate tables go back to 1 January 2024 for DK, DE, AT, NL, BE (all three regions), IE, PT, LT, HU, MK, CH, LI, CZ, PL, SK, SE, LU, GB, EE, NO (every NVDB version of each station), BG (each version of the Тарифа in Държавен вестник), SI (each DarsGo price list, rail surcharge and Karavanke toll from PISRS), the Mont Blanc and Fréjus tunnels and the Storebælt and Øresund bridges, LV, IS (2024 and 2025: the km fee of Lög nr. 87/2004 on trucks and trailers of 10 t or more, each by its own weight, as Skatturinn gives each year's amounts), BY (the tariffs BelToll announced: from 1 June 2023, 20 September 2024 and 1 September 2025, kept by resolution No. 94 from 11 December 2025), RO (the per-day rovinieta of OG 15/2002 until 30 September 2026, with the Danube bridge tolls from 8 September 2025, then TollRo), and RS (the putarina decision's tables as each amendment in Službeni glasnik printed them, in RSD, read by OCR with every price checked against its mirror cell and category IV against III, and held to Putevi Srbije's archived price lists of 10 July 2024 and 1 January 2026). FR goes back to 2024 for the operators whose tariff documents the Internet Archive kept (APRR, AREA, ASF, Cofiroute, Escota, ALIAE, A'liénor, each table names its own), GR to 2024 except Aegean Motorway, HR to 2024 except Bina-Istra, ES (SEITT) to 2025 except the AP-41, TR to 2024 (the motorways whose 2024 tariffs the Internet Archive kept; the Bosphorus bridges' 2024 tariffs weren't), IT to 2025 (the motorway estimate from the Ministry's 2025 sheets of each concession's unit rates, and the Milan ring roads' barriers); the others start at the date in the table above. `tariffs/history_test.go` lists the gaps; `tools/history` rebuilds the past tables from the laws, ECB data and archived copies of official pages.
- **Data updates:** the four data workflows (Tables, History, Croatia, France) rebuild every dataset from its sources each night and compare it with `tariffs/data`. The **Publish data** workflow then commits the datasets whose only change is what OpenStreetMap gives (positions, booth names, the extract's date; `tools/orct/publish.py`); a price or any other change, or a new dataset, is listed in its run summary for review and isn't published. Each Monday, **Data release** makes a patch release (image and binaries) when the data changed and the code didn't. The same run lists, as warnings in its summary, the schemes whose last rates end within 45 days (or ended) with no next version here (`tools/orct/expiring.py`): the new year's tariffs due in December, for instance.
- **Provenance:** each response has a `provenance` block: the release, the Sootcase server and its factor dataset, each dataset used with its file in `tariffs/data` and the OpenStreetMap extract its stations were placed from, the extract of the road classes, the MRV publication of a ferry crossing, the exchange rates, and the defaults taken (`assumed`). With the release and the request, that repeats the result.

## Use

```
go install github.com/majidkabir/carbon-cost/cmd/carbon-cost@latest
carbon-cost calc -h
carbon-cost serve -addr :8090   # web page at /, toll pages at /tolls, POST /v1/carbon-cost, POST /v1/carbon-cost/batch, GET /v1/rates (the datasets), GET /v1/rates/{cc}/{file} (one), GET /v1/places?q=
```

Many trips at once: `calc -csv trips.csv` prices each row of a CSV file whose header names `calc`'s flags without the dash (`from`, `to`, `date`, `cargo-kg`, `mass-kg`, `axles`, `euro`, `co2-class`, `fuel-country`, `via`, `return`, `shipments`, `l-per-100km`, `fuel-bought`, `path`, `country-km`, ...; each row's values on top of the command line's; a month's km by country from the tachographs, one truck a row, is a `country-km` column such as `"DE=3200,PL=4100"`; other columns, a truck or order number, are kept as labels) and prints the rows back with `trip_date`, `distance_km`, `country_km`, `total_eur`, `carbon_eur`, `road_toll_eur`, `fuel_co2_eur`, `ets_eur`, `area_charge_eur`, `ferry_fare_eur`, `fuel_l`, `empty_km`, `shipments` (each shipment's `id=EUR`), `complete`, `missing` and `error` added:

```
$ cat trips.csv
from,to,date,cargo-kg
DKCPH,DEHAM,2026-11-03,20000
NLRTM,ITMIL,2026-11-03,12000
$ carbon-cost calc -csv trips.csv -eua 75 > priced.csv
```

The web page prices trips with stops, shipments, your truck's diesel and a GPX route, keeps saved trucks and the last ten trips in the browser, copies a link that opens and prices the trip, and downloads the result as CSV.

`GET /v1/openapi.json` describes the API (OpenAPI 3.1), every field with its meaning; it is generated from the Go types (`go run ./tools/openapi`), and a test fails when it is out of date. The API takes up to 100 trips in one `POST /v1/carbon-cost/batch` (`{"trips": [<request>, ...]}`) and answers `{"results": [{"result": <response>} or {"error": "..."}, ...]}` in the same order; a trip that can't be priced has its error and doesn't fail the others.

Or with Docker (images are published for each release tag):

```
docker run -p 8090:8090 -e SOOTCASE_URL=https://sootcase.org -e SOOTCASE_API_KEY=... ghcr.io/majidkabir/carbon-cost
```

Release binaries for Linux, macOS and Windows are on the [releases page](https://github.com/majidkabir/carbon-cost/releases). To release, change the version in `RELEASE`; merging that to main publishes it.

### Toll pages

`serve` also publishes a page per country (`/tolls/<cc>`, e.g. `/tolls/de`), an overview (`/tolls`) three pages across countries (`/tolls/zero-emission-trucks`, `/tolls/ets2-diesel`, `/tolls/co2-tax-diesel`) and the ferry crossings with their EU ETS and UK ETS shares by law, their ships and sources (`/tolls/ferry-ets`, in English), with `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt`. Package `site` renders them with Go templates from `tariffs/data`: each rate is priced by the calculator's own code for reference trucks (a 40 t, 5-axle Euro VI truck, by weight, Euro and CO2 class), with the full rate table, the history and the versions to come, zero-emission rules, a worked example and the law behind each version. When the workflows commit new rates, a release updates the pages; nothing is written by hand. Each page has a meta description, a canonical URL, schema.org `Dataset` markup (with the datasets as downloads) and a share image (`/tolls/<cc>.png`, drawn with Inter: `tools/site/font_atlas.py`, `site/font/OFL.txt`). Set `PUBLIC_URL` (or `-public-url`) to the service's public address, default `https://sootcase.org/carbon-cost`: the links, canonical URLs and the sitemap use it. Behind a path (`/carbon-cost`), crawlers read `robots.txt` at the host's root only, so list `Sitemap: <PUBLIC_URL>/sitemap.xml` there or in Google Search Console.

Every page is also in 31 other languages, the languages of the countries covered: `/<lang>/tolls/<cc>` (e.g. `/de/tolls/at`, `/fr/tolls/be`, `/ru/tolls/by`, `/pl/tolls/ets2-diesel`) and `/<lang>/tolls`. Each page links its other languages (`hreflang`, with English as `x-default`) and the sitemap lists them all. The pages are built for the day, the languages in parallel, and kept gzipped (served as such to clients that take gzip); `serve` builds them at start and rebuilds them in the background when the day changes. The words come from message catalogs, one per language in `site/i18n/<lang>.json` with the keys of `en.json` (a missing key falls back to English); numbers, money, percentages and dates follow Unicode CLDR, from `site/i18n/locales.json` (`python3 tools/site/locales.py`, with Babel). The law's own words, rate table names and data notes stay in English, marked so. To add a language, add its CLDR locale to `LANGS` in `tools/site/locales.py`, run it, and add its catalog.

`calc` and `serve` call `https://sootcase.org` by default. Set `SOOTCASE_URL` to use another server, and set `SOOTCASE_API_KEY` to send an API key. The server must run Sootcase v0.9.0 or later, which adds `country_km`.

API request:

```json
{
  "from": {"code": "DKCPH"},
  "to": {"code": "DEHAM"},
  "date": "2026-11-03",
  "cargo_kg": 20000,
  "truck": {"max_mass_kg": 40000, "axles": 5, "euro": 6, "co2_class": 1, "tare_kg": 15000},
  "eua_eur_per_t": 75
}
```

Optional fields: `time` (HH:MM departure, local time where the trip starts; each station's time is taken in its own country's time zone: each toll station is reached that much later at `avg_speed_kmh`, default 70; this prices congestion taxes, the hours a toll is free and the days of time-based charges), `avg_speed_kmh`, `driver_rest`, `vehicle_type` (Sootcase), `tolled_share`, `motorway_share`, `env_zone_km`, `ferry_ets_share`, `ets2_eur_per_t`, `fuel_country`, `fuel_purchases`, `diesel_l` and `l_per_100km` (the truck's diesel), `stops` and `shipments`, `path` (the route driven), `country_km` (no route), `truck.motor_axles`, `truck.motor_mass_kg` and `truck.trailer_mass_kg` (the tractor's own axles and the tractor's and trailer's own permissible maximum masses, for charges that count them), `truck.length_m` (with trailer and load; bridges and ferries by length, default 16.5), `truck.capacity_kg` (the load capacity, the semi-trailer's for a tractor with semi-trailer: Croatia's state ferries charge per tonne of it; without it they aren't priced), `dkk_per_eur`, `fx`, `night`, `compare_co2_classes` (e.g. `[1, 4, 5]`, giving `compare[]` in the response) and `trips_per_year`. Each line of the response carries `source` (the law or tariff) and `sources` (links to it).
