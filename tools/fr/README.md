# French motorway tolls: how the data is gathered

Everything here runs in the **France data** workflow (`.github/workflows/fr-data.yml`, started by pushing to the `fr-data` branch). Its output goes to the `fr-out` branch, and `fr.json` from there is copied to `rates/data/fr-<date>.json`.

1. **Sources** (`sources.json`). France has no national price list: each motorway concessionaire publishes its own tariffs. The operators and their tariff pages come from the list the transport ministry sends the European Commission under article 11 of Directive 1999/62/EC (`source_index`). The legal basis is in `legal`.
2. **Fetch** (`fetch.py`). Downloads each page and the tariff documents linked from it, plus the ministry's toll station dataset from data.gouv.fr. PDFs are converted to text with `pdftotext -layout`. `manifest.json` records every file's URL, sha256, size and fetch time, and any errors.
3. **Parse** (`documents.json`, `parse.py`, `grid.py`). `documents.json` lists the documents that are parsed and their format. `parse.py` writes `pairs.csv`, one row per entry–exit pair, with the class 3 and 4 prices including VAT, the document's URL and sha256, and the date it says it applies from. For text lists it prints the pairs it read next to the price rows in the text, so a format change shows up. VINCI Autoroutes publishes ASF's and Escota's prices as grids, one PDF page per class, with station names written diagonally above the columns and along the diagonal of the triangle, or beside the rows: `grid.py` reads them from the PDF's text positions (`python3 tools/fr/grid.py file.pdf [page]` prints every cell it names, and any cells it couldn't). Checked against VINCI's list of main routes: Lyon–Montpellier, Lyon–Spain, Montpellier–Toulouse, Toulouse–Perthus, Toulouse–Bordeaux, Tours–Bordeaux, Aix–Nice and Aix–Gap match in classes 3 and 4, as does the Escota guide's example (La Saulce–Peyruis, class 1).
4. **Stations** (`stations.py`, `aliases.json`). Puts each tariff station on the map from the ministry's toll station dataset, matching by normalised name, also without "Péage de" and the like, and for "X (Y)" by the Y. `aliases.json` holds the names the dataset spells differently (decided by hand, one line each). If a name is in neither, the script falls back to OpenStreetMap's toll booths and motorway junctions. Where one name matches places far apart, the ones among the most of the operator's other stations are kept. Stations still unplaced that the tariff gives an exit number (the circled numbers in VINCI's grids, the N° column in Cofiroute's list) go to the OpenStreetMap motorway junction with that number nearest the stations they're cheapest to reach, and only if no other junction with that number fits as well. `stations-report.txt` lists the stations it couldn't place: mostly junctions with other networks and the ends of concessions.
5. **Build** (`build.py`). Writes the rate table. All pairs go into one chained toll system: along the route, a truck pays for the published pairs, one after another, that together cover the most of it (usually the longest pair from the first station it passes, then again from where that one ends; an exit's toll booth the route only drives past doesn't cut a longer ticket short). Prices exclude 20% VAT. As a check on the stations' places, it lists pairs whose stations are further apart than their price would pay for.

Covered so far: APRR, AREA, ADELAC (A41 Nord), ALIAE (A79), VINCI Autoroutes' ASF (from 1 June 2026), Escota and Cofiroute, Sanef and SAPN, A'liénor (A65), the Millau viaduct (CEVM, a barrier priced alone) ATMB's A40 (its tickets, and the Cluses-Est and Nangy barriers for trips through; its exits' own booths aren't used), Atlandes (A63) and ALBEA (A150), priced by EURO class, SFTRF's A43 (its square tables per class, `matrix.py`), and Alicorne (A88), ALiS (A28) and the Normandie and Tancarville bridges (CCI Seine-Estuaire). Those three sites refuse scripted requests, so their tables were read in a browser into `browser/*.txt`, each with the page's URL, the date it was read and the date the page gives (Alicorne's page still shows its 1 February 2025 prices; ALiS's gives none). Atlandes and ALBEA publish their prices only as images: `transcribed/*.json` holds them, transcribed by hand, with each image's URL and sha256 (tools/tables checks the images are unchanged). Atlandes' grid is the price per barrier passage, as its calculator shows (21.50 EUR for one barrier, 43.00 for both, class C EURO 6). The operators' through prices for trips onto other networks (ASF and Cofiroute charge some trips to APRR, AREA and Sanef at their exits) are included as published.

**Sanef and SAPN.** Their site answers scripted downloads with a bot check, so their brochures (`2026_02-Grille-Sanef.pdf`, `2026_02-Grille-SAPN.pdf`) are read in a browser: `sanef/extract.js`, pasted into the developer console on Sanef's tariff page, writes each brochure's text positions (pdf.js) with the PDF's URL, size and sha256 at the top, into `sanef/*.items.txt`. Those files are in the repository; `sanef.py` reads their triangles (and the Chambourcy and Montesson barriers, which are priced on their own; Montesson at its base price, not its off-peak one). Sanef's hourly modulation (`grille-modulee-01022026.pdf`) is for class 1 only.

Not covered yet:
- **Arcos (A355, Strasbourg bypass)**. Its prices vary by day, hour and Euro class.

The Fréjus and Mont Blanc tunnels are crossings, in tools/tables.

The rate table for 1 February to 31 May 2026 (`fr-2026-02-01.json`) has APRR, AREA, ADELAC and ALIAE only; from 1 June 2026 (`fr-2026-06-01.json`) it has all the above.

Earlier years (from 1 January 2024) are built by `tools/history/build_fr.py` with these scripts from the operators' tariff documents the Internet Archive kept: each table lists its operators, and the others' motorways aren't priced in it.
